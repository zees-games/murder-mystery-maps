# Writing a map

A map is one JSON file in this directory. A server reads every `.json` here at boot, checks it, and plays it; a file it refuses is listed with the reason, and every other map still plays. Clients never read these files: the server sends the map it is playing to each of them when the map changes.

The format is defined and validated by game-murder-mystery's `game/mm_map_doc.gd`; this file is the mapper's copy of it. It is hide-n-seek's map document with the kind `murder_map` and one spawn group, `players`. The game also reads `hideseek_map`, `prophunt_map` and `playground_map` documents from their own packs, so a map for one of those games can be played here too.

The maps shipped here are generated by [`../tools/build_maps.py`](../tools/build_maps.py) and should be changed there, not here: `tools/build_maps.py --check` fails when a file is not what it would write. The builder is also the quickest way to write a new map, because a wall with its doors and windows and a room with its floor, ceiling and light are one call each. What follows is the format itself.

Every length is metres, every angle degrees, every time seconds. A position is `[x, y, z]`, y is up, and the floor of a building is at y 0.

## The document

```json
{
	"format": 1,
	"kind": "murder_map",
	"id": "mm_my_map",
	"name": "My Map",
	"author": "murder-mystery-maps",
	"blurb": "One line, shown when the map starts.",
	"round_seconds": 300,
	"kill_y": -20.0,
	"environment": {
		"sky_top": [0.30, 0.34, 0.58], "sky_horizon": [0.92, 0.66, 0.52], "ground": [0.30, 0.30, 0.27],
		"sun_pitch": -14, "sun_yaw": 235, "sun_energy": 0.8, "sun_colour": [1.0, 0.76, 0.56],
		"ambient": [0.58, 0.58, 0.70], "ambient_energy": 0.46, "fog": 0.0012
	},
	"boxes": [],
	"props": [],
	"lights": [],
	"doors": [],
	"toys": [],
	"spawns": {"players": []}
}
```

- `id` is lowercase a to z, 0 to 9 and `_`.
- `round_seconds`: how long this map's rounds last; 0 is the server's own, and the server's is always the ceiling.
- `kill_y`: anybody below it has fallen. Put it under anything a player could fall off.
- `environment`: every field is optional. `fog` is a density; 0.0025 is a light haze over a big outdoor map, and 0.008 already hides everything past forty metres. The shipped maps are dusk but readable: keep `ambient_energy` near 0.45 and fog under 0.002.
- `environment` may also carry the map's own fog, in metres: `fog_colour`, `fog_start`, `fog_end` and `fog_max` (the most opaque it gets, 0 to 1), which replaces the haze every map has by default and is written through dot-lighting. The horizon `sky_horizon` should be the fog's colour, since distance fades into the sky. `ambient` tints the shade a quarter of the way.
- `weather` (optional): a dot-weather document (`{"seed": 1, "precipitation": {"kind": "snow", "density": 1.6, "colour": [1, 1, 1], "volumes": [{"min": [x, y, z], "max": [x, y, z]}]}}`), drawn by dot-fx in those boxes only, so nothing falls indoors. It is parsed by dot-weather's own rules; an unknown key refuses the map.
- `ambience` (optional): the bed under the map outdoors, and `ambience_indoor` the one under any ceiling. One of dot-audio's stock beds (all CC0): `amb_wind`, `amb_outdoor`, `amb_night`, `amb_city`, `amb_indoor`, `amb_eerie`, `amb_industrial`, `amb_cave`, `amb_rain`.

## Boxes

The building: floors, walls, ceilings, roofs, glass, a pool, a painted line.

```json
{"at": [0, 1.5, -8], "size": [12, 3, 0.2], "mat": "paint", "tint": [0.95, 0.93, 0.85], "yaw": 0, "solid": true, "visible": true}
```

- `at` is the box's **centre**; `size` is its width, height and depth before `yaw` turns it.
- `mat` is one of: `wood_floor`, `parquet`, `gym`, `tile`, `tile_check`, `tile_small`, `carpet`, `paint`, `plaster`, `ceiling`, `brick`, `stone`, `concrete`, `sidewalk`, `asphalt`, `grass`, `snow`, `dirt`, `sand`, `rubber`, `roof`, `wood`, `metal`, `locker`, `chalkboard`, `whiteboard`, `glass`, `water`, `line`, `hedge`, `fabric`, `flat`, and the photographed generic ids a converted map uses (the ids of dot-map's `source_textures.tsv`): `plaster_painted`, `plaster_worn`, `wallpaper`, `brick_red`, `stone_wall`, `wood_planks`, `tile_floor`, `tile_wall`, `roof_tiles`, `mud`, `door_wood`. In the game `wood_floor`, `parquet`, `carpet`, `concrete`, `ceiling`, `snow`, `grass` and `metal` are photographs too. An unknown name is drawn as `flat`. Textures are laid in world space, so two boxes of the same material meet without a seam.
- `tint` multiplies the material's colour.
- `"solid": false` for anything a player walks through: water, a painted line, a light's glow.
- `"visible": false` for a collider with nothing drawn: an invisible wall at the edge of the map.

## Props

The furniture, the plants, the rocks: everything a player gets behind, around or on top of.

```json
{"id": "furniture_chair", "at": [3.6, 0, 1.2], "yaw": 90, "pitch": 0, "roll": 0}
```

- `id` is a prop from game-murder-mystery's `props/catalogue.json` (`furniture_*` are Kenney's Furniture Kit, `nature_*` the Nature Kit; the file has every id with its size, and `tools/prop_sizes.json` here is a copy the builder uses). An id the game does not have is built as nothing and logged.
- `at` is the middle of the prop's **footprint on whatever it stands on**, so a chair is placed by where it stands and a lamp on a table by the table's top.
- A prop's front faces +Z at yaw 0. So against a wall at the smaller z it faces into the room at yaw 0, against one at the larger z at 180, at the smaller x at 90 and at the larger x at -90.
- Props are solid, to players and to sight. A crouching person fits behind anything about 0.8 m high and 0.6 m across.

## Lights

```json
{"at": [0, 2.9, -4], "range": 9, "energy": 1.1, "colour": [1.0, 0.95, 0.85]}
```

An omni light. Indoors, one per room of about eight by eight metres, just under the ceiling. Browsers draw at most eight lights on any one surface, so a big room wants a few strong lights more than many weak ones.

## Doors

Panels a player opens and shuts with the use key (E): a room's door, a cupboard, a gate, a container's pair, a roller shutter, a panel of wall that slides. A shut door is a wall: it stops a body, a witness's view and a touch, a knife swing. A door is the one thing in a map that moves, and it moves the same on the server and on every client.

```json
{"at": [0, 1.15, -1], "size": [1.37, 2.28, 0.06], "yaw": 0, "motion": "swing", "hinge": "left", "angle": 100,
 "away": true, "open": false, "seconds": 0.6, "travel": 0, "mat": "wood", "tint": [0.55, 0.4, 0.28], "name": "bedroom door", "secret": false}
```

- `at` is the middle of the panel **shut**, and `size` its width, height and thickness, turned by `yaw` like a box. Make it a little smaller than the hole it fills: a centimetre or two round a door, a few millimetres round a secret one.
- `motion` is `swing` (about a hinge at one edge), `slide` (sideways along the wall, into it) or `lift` (straight up, a shutter).
- `hinge` is the edge it swings from, or the way it slides: `left` is the panel's own -x, which with `yaw` 0 is the smaller x and with `yaw` 90 the larger z.
- `angle` is how far a swing opens, in degrees; its sign is the side it opens to. With `away` (the default) the game swings it away from whoever opens it instead, so a door can be opened from either side; set `"away": false` for a door that must only ever go one way (into a room because a sofa is on the other side, out of a stall because the toilet is in).
- `open` is how it stands at the start of every round. `seconds` is how long it takes, and `travel` how far a slide or a lift goes in metres (0 is its whole width or height: a shutter in a tall wall wants less, or it comes out through the roof).
- `name` is what the prompt says: "E  Open the bedroom door".
- `"secret": true` draws no handle. Give it the wall's own `mat` and `tint`, make it nearly the wall's thickness, and slide it: it reads as wall until somebody tries it.
- A door never opens or shuts into anybody: the use is refused while a body stands anywhere it will pass through.

game-murder-mystery's map check swings every door both ways it can go and fails one that passes through a wall or a prop, and walks a ray through every doorway with its door open and fails one with something standing in it. In the builder, a door is a hole in a wall with a fifth element: `(3.0, 4.4, 0.0, DOOR_H, {"name": "bedroom door"})`.

## Toys

```json
"toys": [
	{"toy": "beach_ball", "at": [16.5, 0, -2.5]},
	{"toy": "swing", "at": [-17, 0, 6], "yaw": 90},
	{"toy": "lamp_post", "at": [13.5, 0, -5]},
	{"toy": "light_switch", "at": [12, 0, -3], "links": [2]}
]
```

Things players play with, from dot-props' toy catalogue: `beach_ball`, `football`, `bowling_ball`, `bowling_pin`, `barrel`, `tyre`, `punching_bag`, `swing`, `seesaw`, `merry_go_round`, `wobbly_platform`, `trampoline`, `bounce_pad`, `light_switch`, `lamp_post`, `fountain`, `horn`, `bell`. The server simulates them and every client sees them where the server has them. At most 48 on a map.

- `at` is the middle of the toy's foot on the floor; `yaw` turns it (a swing's bar runs along its x; a bounce pad throws along its +z, where its arrow points).
- `links` (a switch only): the indexes in this list of the toys it turns on and off. `on` starts a lamp or a fountain off or on.
- A ball, a pin, a barrel or a tyre is pushed by whoever walks into it, and is no wall: nobody can be stuck on one. Everything else is solid, and the stand-ins' walkable mesh goes round it.
- Every toy goes home at the start of a round.
- A toy the server's dot-props does not have is left out (and the server's map listing says so); the map still plays.
- Keep a trampoline seven metres clear and a bounce pad's landing seven metres clear along its arrow: a trampoline throws a person 2.3 m up, so beside a low roof it would put them somewhere the map meant to be reached another way, or not at all.

In the builder, `m.toy("swing", x, z, yaw)` places one and moves it to the nearest clear, floored spot within `near` metres (it stops the build when there is none); `lamps_on_a_switch(m, lamps, switch)` lights a path. Each map's toys are its own `toys_<map>` function, near the end of the builder. `tools/build_maps.py --spots mm_motel` prints a plan of the open floor to choose from.

## Spawns

```json
"spawns": {
	"players": [{"at": [-5, 0.05, 0], "yaw": 90}, {"at": [7, 0.05, 0], "yaw": -90}]
}
```

One group, `players`. `at` is where the player's feet go, and a round starts with everybody apart, so spread at least sixteen spawns over different rooms and yards, upstairs and down; with more players than spots, later ones are placed in rings round the first. Keep spawns clear of props: the game's map check fails a spawn with no floor under it or with something in the way, and the builder moves its own.

## Checking a map

```bash
python3 tools/build_maps.py --check        # every document is what the builder writes
python3 tools/build_maps.py --spots mm_motel   # a plan of the open floor, to place toys and spawns
```

Then run game-murder-mystery's map check, which fits each map on the wire, looks up every prop, finds a floor under every spawn, swings every door and walks the stairs.

## Stairs and crawl spaces

There is no stair or ramp in the format: a flight is boxes. Each step is a slab about 0.22 m higher than the last (a player steps up 0.4 m) and about 0.3 m deep rather than a block down to the floor, so there is room under a flight for the flight below it in a stairwell, or for a dark corner under the stairs. A crawl space is a gap 1.1 m high: a person crouched is 0.95 m and fits, standing is 1.8 m and does not. The builder has `flight()` for stairs.
